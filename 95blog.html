Patricia Highsmith had anorexia as a teenager and episodes of depression throughout her life. Despite her literary success, she wrote in her diary in January 1970: 'am now cynical, fairly rich ... lonely, depressed, and totally pessimistic.' She was an alcoholic who by middle age drank from breakfast till bedtime. She smoked 40 Gauloises cigarettes a day and rarely ate fruit or vegetables.

Acquaintances describe her public and private behavior as often eccentric, rude, difficult, and antisocial. She brought her pet snails to one dinner party and let them wander over the mahogany. At one dinner party in 1968 she deliberately lowered her head to a candle and set her hair on fire. She had two friends as houseguests in 1971 and threw a dead rat into their room. Those who knew her suggested this behavior might have resulted from depression, alcoholism, Asperger syndrome, or a personality disorder.

Highsmith lived alone most of her adult life, saying in a 1991 interview, 'I choose to live alone because my imagination functions better when I don't have to speak with people'.


Sexuality:
Highsmith called herself 'basically polygamous' and was consistently unfaithful to her lovers. She wrote, 'there is something perverted within me, that I don't love a girl anymore if she loves me more than I love her.' According to biographer Andrew Wilson, 'She would be forever prone to falling in love but always happiest when alone.'

August 24. 2026 Wedding poem

On the happiest day of my life
Emma led a man down the steps of her apartment
covered in a long coat
to obscure
his body completely held in red rope.

Earlier I was laid in wet shells
at the bottom of a bathtub
someone else's hair hanging from my ears
as I contemplated the white ceiling.

I didn't even imagine
on this one day of all others
that someone could not love
the silk change
that had befallen us.

I thought of them at the beach,
playing in the rocks like crabs
as the water came
their fabrics now built into the body,
hallways of calcium shells hardened
through intricacy.

Emma had told me that she wished to leave no mark
she deleted her photos every so often
spines and fur grew or receded
through her sharp hand, her clear voice
speaking always as though
there was no way to not be brave.

Meanwhile,
Elliot taught me to be a man
'it is more than fashion' he said,
it is an ease, a belief in me
in himself, and all others
a belief in a world that was safe for us
made beautiful through our own mechanisms
the softness and care of our speech,
our openness and reflex
to look, to wonder

They came like angels to the edge of the tub and looked
over.

I saw a love that would always surround me.

They said they were most excited for after
when they would run through a meadow
and just look into each other's eyes

I felt that I risked Emma's fears of perception
to tell her how much I thought of this moment too,
the grass and the meadow flies all around
a hollow in the depth and assurance of time
the meadow would change but not end,
we did not have to be afraid,
for we could make what we needed
from our own hands.

Elliot was guided slowly down the stairway
giant heels thumping with each step
in the garden
he slipped out of the coat
in the low dusk
he and Emma stalked to the fence
arranging the tendrils of rope
until they were fluid
faces from every window of the courtyard watched as
we walked as ourselves.
